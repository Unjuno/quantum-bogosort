\section{Adaptive-Agent Mechanism}

A central modeling question is whether positive outcome--accessibility alignment must be inserted exogenously. We instead consider an adapted agent with environment state $X_t$, internal belief state $B_t$, scalar evaluation signal $Y_t=h(B_t)$, and later outcome $U_T$.

Statistical dependence alone is not sufficient for the QBS mean-shift mechanism. The relevant directional object is the conditional mean
\[
m(y)=\mathbb E[U_T\mid Y_t=y].
\]
In the clean score-measurable case,
\[
S_t=s(Y_t),
\]
with $S_t\ge0$, $0<\mathbb E[S_t]<\infty$, and the required integrability. Supplementary Theorem S2 gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(U_T,S_t)
=
\operatorname{Cov}(m(Y_t),s(Y_t)).
\]
If $m(y)$ and $s(y)$ are both nondecreasing on the support of $Y_t$, then
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(U_T,S_t)\ge0.
\]
Strict comovement on a positive-probability set gives strict positivity. By the main covariance identity, positive covariance produces positive first-person mean uplift under the weighted-measure model.

A particularly transparent calibration case occurs when the score is a posterior-mean forecast from internal information $B_t$:
\[
Y_t=\mathbb E[U_T\mid B_t].
\]
The tower property then gives
\[
\mathbb E[U_T\mid Y_t]
=
Y_t.
\]
Thus Supplementary Corollary S2.2 makes the directional-calibration premise automatic for a true posterior-mean score. Any nondecreasing score-measurable accessibility map then inherits the sign conclusion of S2.

For learned scores, exact calibration is not assumed. Writing
\[
e(Y_t)=\mathbb E[U_T\mid Y_t]-Y_t,
\]
S2.3--S2.4 bound the damage caused by calibration error using either its variance or ordinary prediction MSE. These are sufficient population bounds rather than necessary conditions. Their finite-sample counterparts are developed in the Appendix: S2.5--S2.10 cover independent held-out certification, training and finite-candidate selection validity, a generic moment-envelope interface, and bounded, light-tail, and median-of-means instantiations. These statistical layers are kept separate from the conceptual mechanism because they concern validation technology rather than the definition of QBS.

For square-integrable $U_T$, the quantity
\[
M(U_T;Y_t)
=
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[U_T\mid Y_t])
\]
measures conditional-mean predictive strength. Positive mutual information $I(U_T;Y_t)>0$ is weaker: dependence can occur through conditional variance while $\mathbb E[U_T\mid Y_t]$ remains constant. In that case every accessibility map measurable with respect to $Y_t$ has zero covariance with $U_T$.

The intended mechanism is therefore
\[
\text{adaptation / inference}
\rightarrow
\text{predictive conditional mean}
\rightarrow
\text{aligned accessibility}
\rightarrow
\operatorname{Cov}(U_T,S_t)>0.
\]
The next section removes the restrictive assumption that accessibility must be a deterministic function of the score and keeps the residual outcome--accessibility dependence explicit.

Experiment E2 tests premise generation in a nonlinear toy environment: a misspecified model that cannot represent the relevant interaction fails to produce predictive ordering, while an interaction-capable model succeeds. E3 supplies a paired endogenous-policy example. These classical simulations support the modeled mechanism; neither they nor the S2 statistical certificates establish a physical Everettian accessibility map.
