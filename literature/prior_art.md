# Prior-Art Ledger

This file records the main literature families relevant to QBS and the current novelty boundary. It is a working research ledger, not a finished literature review.

## 1. Everettian probability and decision theory

### David Wallace — decision-theoretic Born rule

**Source:** David Wallace, "A formal proof of the Born rule from decision-theoretic assumptions," arXiv:0906.2718 (2009); subsequently published in revised-title form as "How to Prove the Born Rule" in *Many Worlds? Everett, Quantum Theory, and Reality* (Oxford University Press, 2010).

**What it establishes:** a formal decision-theoretic derivation of Born-rule weights within Everettian quantum mechanics under explicit rationality assumptions.

**Relation to QBS:** both frameworks connect branching and rational evaluation, but QBS does not attempt to derive the Born rule. QBS treats the base measure as given and studies a separate policy-dependent accessibility weighting and recognition-dependent trajectory map.

**Difference:** QBS's core decomposition separates ordinary causal policy effects from first-person conditioning; that is not the target of the Wallace derivation.

### Hilary Greaves — caring measure in a deterministic multiverse

**Source:** Hilary Greaves, "Understanding Deutsch's probability in a deterministic multiverse," *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part B* 35(3), 423–456 (2004), DOI `10.1016/j.shpsb.2004.04.006`; earlier arXiv:quant-ph/0312136.

**What it establishes:** an interpretation of Everettian decision-theoretic weights in terms of rational caring across future branches rather than ordinary subjective uncertainty.

**Relation to QBS:** both distinguish deterministic branching from ordinary stochastic ignorance and assign branch-sensitive normative/first-person structure.

**Difference:** QBS models accessibility as an explicit nonnegative weighting function and allows recognition-dependent policy selection to change the trajectory map and accessibility map.

### Simon Saunders — chance in Everett

**Sources:** Simon Saunders, "Chance in the Everett Interpretation," in *Many Worlds? Everett, Quantum Theory, and Reality*, pp. 181–205 (Oxford University Press, 2010), DOI `10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199560561.003.0008`; and "The Everett Interpretation: Probability," in *The Routledge Companion to Philosophy of Physics*, pp. 230–246 (Routledge, 2021), DOI `10.4324/9781315623818-21`.

**What they establish:** accounts of branching and physical chance/probability in Everettian quantum mechanics, with squared amplitudes playing the relevant probabilistic role.

**Relation to QBS:** these works are important for the question of what probability and chance mean in an Everettian branching structure.

**Difference:** QBS currently does not derive its accessibility map from physical chance. The Everett-QBS bridge remains an explicit open assumption.

## 2. Self-locating uncertainty

### Charles Sebens and Sean Carroll

**Source:** Charles T. Sebens and Sean M. Carroll, "Self-Locating Uncertainty and the Origin of Probability in Everettian Quantum Mechanics," *The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 69(1), 25–74 (2018), DOI `10.1093/bjps/axw004`; earlier arXiv:1405.7577.

**What it establishes:** a self-locating-uncertainty approach to Everettian credence and a route to Born-rule probabilities, including cases with multiple similar observers.

**Relation to QBS:** this is one of the closest conceptual antecedents because QBS also distinguishes third-person branch structure from first-person conditioning.

**Difference:** QBS adds a recognition/information state that can change policy selection and studies policy-dependent trajectory and accessibility changes jointly.

## 3. Quantum suicide / immortality literature

### Max Tegmark

**Source:** Max Tegmark, "The Interpretation of Quantum Mechanics: Many Worlds or Many Words?", arXiv:quant-ph/9709032 (1997); published in *Fortschritte der Physik* 46(6–8), 855–862 (1998), DOI `10.1002/(SICI)1521-3978(199811)46:6/8<855::AID-PROP855>3.0.CO;2-Q`.

**What it contributes:** discussion of a quantum-suicide thought experiment as a proposed distinction in how an observer might reason under a Many-Worlds interpretation.

**Relation to QBS:** this is historical background for extreme observer-selection intuitions.

**Difference:** QBS abstracts the thought experiment into general termination/accessibility mathematics and policy dependence.

### Jacques Mallah

**Source:** Jacques Mallah, "Many-Worlds Interpretations Can Not Imply 'Quantum Immortality'," arXiv:0902.0187 (2009).

**What it argues:** Many-Worlds by itself does not imply certain survival or quantum immortality.

**Importance for QBS:** this is a key limitation/counterpoint. QBS must not present observer weighting as establishing certain survival or as a theorem of MWI.

## 4. Observer-selection mechanisms in Everett

### Robin Hanson — mangled worlds

**Source:** Robin D. Hanson, "When Worlds Collide: Quantum Probability From Observer Selection?", *Foundations of Physics* 33(7), 1129–1150 (2003), DOI `10.1023/A:1025642019178`; earlier arXiv:quant-ph/0108070.

**What it proposes:** a physically motivated observer-selection mechanism in which small worlds can become effectively unobservable through imperfect decoherence interactions, potentially reproducing Born-like outcome statistics.

**Relation to QBS:** both consider observer selection over an Everettian branch structure.

**Difference:** Hanson's mechanism is a physical proposal tied to decoherence/world interaction. QBS currently uses an abstract policy-dependent accessibility map and explicitly lacks a comparable physical derivation.

## 5. Anthropic decision theory

### Stuart Armstrong

**Source:** Stuart Armstrong, "Anthropic decision theory," arXiv:1110.6437 (2011).

**What it establishes:** a decision-theoretic treatment of anthropic/self-locating problems that focuses on what agents should do rather than first requiring a unique anthropic probability assignment.

**Relation to QBS:** both emphasize policy choice under self-locating structure.

**Difference:** QBS introduces a policy-dependent accessibility measure and exact decomposition between trajectory changes and conditioning changes.

## 6. Classical ascertainment and weighted distributions

### Fisher, Rao, and Patil--Rao

**Sources:**
- R. A. Fisher, "The effect of methods of ascertainment upon the estimation of frequencies," *Annals of Eugenics* 6(1), 13–25 (1934), DOI `10.1111/j.1469-1809.1934.tb02105.x`.
- C. R. Rao, "On discrete distributions arising out of methods of ascertainment," *Sankhya: The Indian Journal of Statistics, Series A* 27, 311–324 (1965).
- G. P. Patil and C. R. Rao, "Weighted Distributions and Size-Biased Sampling with Applications to Wildlife Populations and Human Families," *Biometrics* 34(2), 179–189 (1978), DOI `10.2307/2530008`.

**What this literature establishes:** unequal probabilities of being observed or recorded transform the encountered distribution into a weighted distribution. Patil and Rao explicitly study general weight functions and size-biased sampling.

**Direct relation to QBS:** the normalized QBS first-person law is not merely analogous to this construction. Under bounded accessibility it can be represented exactly as ordinary state-dependent ascertainment conditioning, and for a general integrable nonnegative weight it has an exact classical record-size-bias representation. See [`../supplementary/selection_equivalence.md`](../supplementary/selection_equivalence.md).

**Difference:** this classical equivalence concerns the weighted probability law, not the physical ontology or causal mechanism. QBS can retain distinctive content only through the recognition-dependent policy/trajectory/accessibility architecture or through additional physical/interventional predictions that distinguish an Everett bridge from a behavior-matched classical selection mechanism.

## 7. Current novelty hypothesis

The strongest novelty claim should not be that QBS invented observer selection, self-location, Everettian decision theory, or weighted encountered distributions. Those have substantial prior literatures.

The current candidate novelty is the combined formal structure:

1. recognition/information state can change which policy is selected, without assigning recognition a privileged physical causal power;
2. the selected policy changes the mapping from primitive branch randomness to trajectories;
3. policy may also change observer-indexed accessibility;
4. the resulting first-person value admits an exact trajectory-versus-conditioning decomposition;
5. the marginal QBS contribution has an exact interaction decomposition with ordinary adaptive policy;
6. shared recognition and shared world structure can be analyzed separately from single-observer marginal first-person uplift;
7. the framework explicitly exposes the classical selection-equivalence null, so a specifically Everettian claim must add a physical derivation or distinguishable predictions beyond the weighted law itself.

This novelty claim remains provisional until the literature review is broader.
