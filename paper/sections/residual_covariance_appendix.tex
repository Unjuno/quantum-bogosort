\section{Residual Conditional-Covariance Extension}

This section records Supplementary Theorem S2.11, which removes the deterministic score-measurability requirement $S=s(Y)$.

Let $U$ and $S$ be square-integrable random variables with $S\ge0$ and $0<\mathbb E[S]<\infty$. Let $Y$ be any conditioning signal and define
\[
m(Y)=\mathbb E[U\mid Y],
\qquad
a(Y)=\mathbb E[S\mid Y].
\]
Square integrability guarantees that all covariance and residual terms below are finite.

\begin{theorem}[Residual conditional-covariance alignment]
The exact decomposition is
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(U,S)
=
\operatorname{Cov}(m(Y),a(Y))
+
\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(U,S\mid Y)].
\]
If $m(Y)$ and $a(Y)$ are comonotone, then
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(m(Y),a(Y))\ge0.
\]
If, for some $\varepsilon\ge0$,
\[
\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(U,S\mid Y)]
\ge-\varepsilon,
\]
then
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(U,S)
\ge
\operatorname{Cov}(m(Y),a(Y))-\varepsilon.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(m(Y),a(Y))>\varepsilon
\]
is sufficient for $\operatorname{Cov}(U,S)>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first identity is the law of total covariance. For an independent copy $Y'$,
\[
2\operatorname{Cov}(m(Y),a(Y))
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
(m(Y)-m(Y'))(a(Y)-a(Y'))
\right].
\]
Comonotonicity makes the integrand nonnegative almost surely, proving the sign of the conditional-mean covariance. The residual lower bound then gives the stated total lower bound by direct addition.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Nonnegative residual dependence]
If
\[
\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(U,S\mid Y)]\ge0
\]
and $m(Y)$ and $a(Y)$ are comonotone, then
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(U,S)\ge0.
\]
Strict positivity follows if either component is strictly positive.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Residual representation.}
Define
\[
\eta=U-m(Y),
\qquad
\xi=S-a(Y).
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E[\eta\mid Y]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\xi\mid Y]=0,
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(U,S)
=
\operatorname{Cov}(m(Y),a(Y))
+
\mathbb E[\eta\xi],
\]
where
\[
\mathbb E[\eta\xi]
=
\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(U,S\mid Y)].
\]
Thus S2 is recovered as the special case in which $S$ is $Y$-measurable and the residual accessibility variable $\xi$ vanishes.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
Comonotone conditional means are not sufficient if the residual term is strongly negative. For a bounded explicit example, let $Y$ be constant and let $\eta$ be Rademacher, with $P(\eta=1)=P(\eta=-1)=1/2$. Set
\[
U=\eta,
\qquad
S=M-\eta,
\qquad
M>1.
\]
Then $S>0$ almost surely, the conditional means are constant, and the residuals satisfy $\xi=-\eta$. Hence the conditional-mean covariance is zero while
\[
\mathbb E[\eta\xi]=-1<0.
\]
Thus the residual term cannot be omitted once score-measurability is relaxed.
