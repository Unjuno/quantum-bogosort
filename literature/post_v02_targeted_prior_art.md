# Post-v0.2 Targeted Prior-Art Audit

**Search snapshot:** 2026-08-18  
**Scope:** direct-overlap search after the S2.11–S2.13 general-accessibility development

This note records a narrow search for literature that could materially weaken the current QBS novelty framing. It is not a claim of exhaustive literature coverage.

## H — overlap hypothesis

The highest-risk novelty overlap would be prior work that combines most of the following in one formal structure:

1. an information/recognition state causally changes policy;
2. policy changes downstream trajectories;
3. policy or observer state also changes a self-location / observer-selection weighting;
4. first-person value is evaluated under that weighting;
5. ordinary trajectory and observer-weighting effects are explicitly decomposed;
6. general accessibility retains a residual conditional-dependence term rather than assuming deterministic score-measurability.

The search therefore targeted self-locating decision theory, observer selection, Everettian credence, policy optimization with copies, and policy-dependent probability/weighting.

## T — sources with highest relevance

### Robert Garisto — *How to Select Observers* (2020)

**Source:** arXiv:2005.12342.

Garisto develops a general formalism for observer-selection effects and emphasizes that the result depends strongly on how the observer is selected and how enclosing sets/reference classes are specified. In particular, the paper distinguishes selection by "being in" a set from selection by "picking from" a set and shows that observer-selection effects can change or disappear when those assumptions change.

**QBS consequence:** observer weighting cannot be treated as automatic merely because an observer exists in a multiverse. Any QBS bridge must specify the selection protocol represented by `S`, and apparently favorable observer-selection effects cannot validate that protocol by themselves.

**Overlap level:** medium conceptual overlap on observer-selection formalism; low overlap on recognition-dependent policy/trajectory decomposition.

### Peter J. Lewis — *Probability, Self-Location, and Quantum Branching* (2009)

**Source:** *Philosophy of Science* 76(5), 1009–1019, DOI 10.1086/605805.

Lewis argues that if probability is used in Everett it must be connected to self-location, but also argues that branching structure may not supply enough structure to determine the relevant self-location probabilities and that decision-theoretic arguments do not automatically close that gap.

**QBS consequence:** the Everett-QBS accessibility bridge remains an independent problem even if the decision-theoretic framework is internally consistent.

**Overlap level:** strong boundary relevance; not a direct policy/accessibility decomposition overlap.

### Jake Khawaja — *Conquering Mount Everett: Branch Counting Versus the Born Rule* (2026)

**Source:** *The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 77(2), DOI 10.1086/726282.

Khawaja develops indexed branch-counting as a rationally motivated self-locating credence rule and argues that it can remain a viable competitor to Born-rule credence assignments.

**QBS consequence:** contemporary Everett literature still contains live disagreement over the rational self-locating measure. QBS must therefore avoid presenting its accessibility measure as fixed by Everettian branching alone.

**Overlap level:** strong physical-bridge relevance; low direct overlap with recognition-dependent policy decomposition.

### Cooper, Oesterheld, and Conitzer — modified anthropics and ex ante policy optimization (2024)

**Source:** arXiv:2411.04462.

This paper directly links self-locating beliefs to policy choice and characterizes conditions under which modified anthropic beliefs can make causal decision theory recover ex ante optimal policies.

**QBS consequence:** no novelty claim should rest on the generic proposition that self-location or copy structure can change recommended policy.

**Overlap level:** high on self-location/policy linkage; still distinct from QBS's joint trajectory/accessibility and residual-dependence decompositions.

### Stuart Armstrong — anthropic decision theory (2011)

**Source:** arXiv:1110.6437.

Anthropic decision theory treats action choice directly in self-locating problems rather than requiring a unique anthropic probability assignment first.

**QBS consequence:** decision theory over copies/observers is established prior art.

**Overlap level:** high background overlap, but not direct overlap with the current QBS decomposition stack.

## D — direct-overlap assessment

The targeted search did **not** identify a source that, in one framework, clearly reproduces all of the following QBS structure:

$$
R
\longrightarrow
\pi_R
\longrightarrow
(U_R,S_R),
$$

with an exact recognition decomposition separating ordinary trajectory and first-person conditioning effects, a separate policy--selection interaction decomposition, and the general-accessibility identity:

$$
\mathrm{Cov}(U,S)
=
\mathrm{Cov}(E[U\mid Y],E[S\mid Y])
+
E[\mathrm{Cov}(U,S\mid Y)].
$$

This is **not** evidence that no such prior work exists. It means that the present targeted search did not find a direct structural duplicate.

## C — novelty claim after this search

The manuscript should continue to avoid claiming novelty for:

- observer selection in general;
- self-locating credence;
- anthropic decision theory;
- branch-sensitive policy choice;
- self-location affecting actions;
- normalized weighting / change of measure;
- covariance or total-covariance identities;
- Cauchy--Schwarz, Hoeffding, Bernstein, or median-of-means tools;
- alternative Everettian credence rules.

The defensible provisional contribution is the **combined framework architecture**:

1. recognition is modeled as a causal policy input;
2. policy changes the trajectory map on common primitive randomness;
3. policy may also change observer-indexed accessibility;
4. trajectory and first-person conditioning effects are exactly separated;
5. policy and QBS conditioning have an explicit interaction decomposition;
6. predictive conditional-mean alignment supplies an endogenous agent mechanism;
7. general accessibility retains residual outcome--accessibility dependence explicitly;
8. marginal first-person uplift is distinguished from cross-copy policy coherence;
9. statistical certification is kept separate from the physical Everett bridge.

## U — remaining search risk

The highest-value unresolved literature searches are now very specific:

- causal models in which an action/policy changes the reference class or self-location measure itself;
- observer-selection frameworks with endogenous selection weights tied to policy or observer persistence;
- formal decompositions of causal outcome effects versus anthropic/self-location reweighting effects;
- decision-dependent probability measures in formal decision theory that may be mathematically equivalent to the QBS `S_\pi` construction;
- recent 2025–2026 Everett/self-location papers that explicitly couple observer weighting to agent policy.

A broad additional search for generic anthropic decision theory is lower value because substantial overlap is already established and acknowledged.

## Manuscript action taken

The manuscript Related Work section now cites Garisto, Lewis, and Khawaja alongside the existing Everett, observer-selection, and anthropic decision-theory literature.

The intended effect is to strengthen the boundary language, not to inflate the novelty claim.

## ERROR CHECK

1. This audit does not claim exhaustive prior-art coverage.
2. Failure to find a direct duplicate is not evidence of absence.
3. Standard probability identities are not claimed as novel.
4. Self-location changing policy is explicitly acknowledged as prior art.
5. Observer-selection assumptions are treated as model inputs requiring justification.
6. Everettian branching is not treated as uniquely fixing the QBS accessibility map.
7. The physical bridge remains independent of theorem correctness.
8. No new theorem or experiment is introduced by this literature update.

## Current conclusion

**NO DIRECT STRUCTURAL DUPLICATE WAS FOUND IN THIS TARGETED SEARCH, BUT THE SEARCH FURTHER NARROWS THE CLAIM: THE POTENTIAL CONTRIBUTION IS THE COMBINED RECOGNITION/POLICY/TRAJECTORY/ACCESSIBILITY/RESIDUAL-DECOMPOSITION ARCHITECTURE, NOT OBSERVER SELECTION OR SELF-LOCATING DECISION THEORY BY THEMSELVES.**
