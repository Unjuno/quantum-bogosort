# Multi-Observer Normalization

## Motivation

A common confusion is to treat different observers' first-person probabilities as if they were shares of one common probability budget. The model does not require that.

## Definitions

For observer index `i`, let `S_i` be that observer's accessibility weight. Define the observer-specific first-person measure by:

```math
d\mu_i^{FP}
=
\frac{S_i}{E[S_i]}
\,d\mu.
```

## Result

Each observer's first-person measure is normalized separately. Therefore different observers' favorable-event probabilities do not have to sum to one across observers.

## Interpretation

In a shared zero-sum world, several observers can each have a high first-person probability of their own favorable outcome even when the corresponding favorable events are mutually exclusive at the shared-world level.

This is not a contradiction because the first-person measures have different denominators.

## Experiment

Historical multi-observer simulations tested this separation between shared-world event probability and separately normalized observer-indexed probabilities. Those exploratory outputs are not part of E1–E5 but are retained in the research archive/history.

## Limitations

The result follows once separate accessibility functions are admitted. It does not establish how physical observers in Everett should be assigned such functions.
