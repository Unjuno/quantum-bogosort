\section{Related Work}

The QBS framework sits at the intersection of several established literatures. Everettian decision-theoretic work derives or interprets Born-rule weights under rationality assumptions, including Wallace's formal decision-theoretic proof \cite{wallace2009born} and Greaves's caring-measure interpretation \cite{greaves2003deutsch}. Work by Sebens and Carroll develops self-locating uncertainty as a basis for Everettian credence \cite{sebenscarroll2014selflocating}, while Saunders develops accounts of chance, branch counting, and physical probability in branching theories \cite{saunders2016chance,saunders2021probability,saunders2022branchcounting,saunders2026physicalprobability}.

The decision-theoretic program is contested. Price argues that the analogy with ordinary decision under uncertainty is not automatic \cite{price2006probability,price2008decisions}, while Kent emphasizes that branch weights, rational decision roles, and empirical confirmation are logically distinct problems \cite{kent2009oneworld}. Ara\'ujo's comparison of deterministic many-world theories likewise illustrates that the relevant probability measure can depend on the underlying dynamics \cite{araujo2018deterministic}. These critiques support the present manuscript's decision to keep the abstract first-person change of measure separate from any physical Everett identification.

Quantum-suicide discussions provide historical background for extreme observer-selection intuitions \cite{tegmark1997manywords}, but Many-Worlds alone does not establish certain survival or quantum immortality \cite{mallah2009immortality}. This manuscript therefore treats such thought experiments as background rather than as a theorem or implementation target.

Observer-selection proposals such as Hanson's mangled-worlds mechanism are closer to a physical selection model \cite{hanson2001mangled}, but their mechanism differs sharply from the abstract accessibility map used here. Anthropically motivated decision theories, including Armstrong's anthropic decision theory \cite{armstrong2011anthropic}, provide another nearby literature in which policy choice is studied under self-locating structure.

The normalized weighting operation itself also belongs to a broad classical change-of-measure and importance-sampling literature; weighted empirical measures are standard objects in that setting \cite{hultnyquist2012importance}. Accordingly, neither normalized weighting nor the elementary covariance identity is presented as the central novelty of QBS.

The provisional novelty claim is narrower: QBS combines recognition-dependent policy change, policy-dependent trajectory and accessibility maps, an exact decomposition of ordinary trajectory effects from first-person conditioning, a separate policy--QBS interaction decomposition, endogenous predictor/accessibility alignment in adaptive toy agents, and an explicit distinction between single-observer marginal uplift and branch-wide policy coherence. This positioning remains subject to revision as the literature review expands.
