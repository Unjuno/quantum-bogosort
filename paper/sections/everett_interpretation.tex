\section{Everett Interpretation}

The mathematical framework becomes an Everettian observer-selection model only after an additional physical identification. We state this explicitly as the Everett--QBS bridge assumption:
\[
d\mu^{\mathrm{FP}}_\pi(\omega)
=
\frac{S_\pi(\omega)}{\mathbb E_\mu[S_\pi]}
\,d\mu(\omega).
\]
Under this assumption, policy-dependent accessibility acts as an observer-indexed self-location weight over the base branch measure.

The present work does not derive this bridge from unitary quantum mechanics, decoherence theory, observer dynamics, or the Born rule. The bridge may therefore be accepted, modified, or rejected independently of the measure-theoretic theorems. Rejecting it removes the Everettian physical interpretation but leaves the probability identities and classical policy simulations intact.

The physical status of the bridge should be evaluated in layers. At the abstract level, $S_\pi$ is a nonnegative normalizable change-of-measure weight. At an observer-model level, one would need an independent account of why observer persistence, records, or self-location produce that weight. At the strongest physical level, one would need to explain the relation between the weight, decoherence, branch amplitudes, Born-rule probability, and operational quantum predictions.

The project does not identify a utility-favoring weight with physical accessibility merely because it increases first-person value. A serious Everettian model must derive or constrain $S_\pi$ independently of the outcomes it is intended to favor and must remain invariant under physically equivalent branch descriptions.

There is also an empirical distinction. If a concrete accessibility model predicts deviations from standard observer frequencies or other operational statistics, those deviations provide tests. If all operational predictions agree with standard Everettian quantum mechanics and the proposal changes only the interpretation of self-location, then ordinary experiments may not uniquely identify the bridge. Such a proposal should be described as an interpretive/self-location extension rather than independently confirmed new physics.

The detailed support, constraint, and rejection criteria are recorded in `docs/everett_bridge_tests.md` and summarized in the limitations section. This separation is methodologically important: the core results concern how a weighted first-person measure behaves once an accessibility function is specified and how recognition-dependent policies can change both trajectories and accessibility; the unresolved question is whether Everettian observer physics selects such a function.
