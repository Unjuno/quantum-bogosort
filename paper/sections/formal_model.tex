\section{Formal Model}

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mu)$ be a base probability space representing primitive branch randomness or histories. Recognition state is $R\in\{0,1\}$ and selects a policy $\pi_R$. The policy induces a trajectory map and an accessibility map,
\[
R\longrightarrow \pi_R\longrightarrow (U_R,S_R),
\]
where $U_R:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ is trajectory utility and $S_R:\Omega\to[0,\infty)$ is observer-indexed accessibility.

For each recognition state we assume
\[
0<\mathbb E_\mu[S_R]<\infty,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\mu[|U_R|]<\infty,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\mu[|U_R|S_R]<\infty.
\]
The separate base and weighted integrability conditions ensure that both ordinary expectations and covariance contributions are finite; neither condition implies the other in general. The first-person measure is
\[
\mu_R^{\mathrm{FP}}(A)
=
\frac{\mathbb E_\mu[\mathbf 1_A S_R]}{\mathbb E_\mu[S_R]},
\]
and first-person value is
\[
V_R
=
\frac{\mathbb E_\mu[U_RS_R]}{\mathbb E_\mu[S_R]}.
\]

The same change of measure applies to a present-state descriptor when accessibility is determined over a future continuation. Let $Z$ denote a present state and let $S_T$ denote future-continuation accessibility. Then
\[
P_{\mathrm{FP}}(Z\in A)
=
\frac{\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_{\{Z\in A\}}S_T]}{\mathbb E[S_T]},
\]
and, for a discrete state,
\[
P_{\mathrm{FP}}(Z=z)
=
\frac{\mathbb E[S_T\mid Z=z]P(Z=z)}{\mathbb E[S_T]}.
\]
Thus differential expected future accessibility can reweight present self-location. This is a conditioning/change-of-measure statement; it does not imply backward causation or a causal modification of the base law of $Z$.

For paired counterfactual comparisons, the preferred representation uses a common primitive realization $z$ and policy-dependent histories $\omega_R(z)$. This prevents a recognition comparison from being confused with a comparison of unrelated Monte Carlo samples.

Define the normalized covariance contribution
\[
Q(U,S)
=
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(U,S)}{\mathbb E[S]}.
\]
The formal development distinguishes changes in the trajectory utility map from changes in the accessibility map. In particular, recognition need not merely reweight a fixed set of realized utilities: a changed policy may induce $U_1\neq U_0$ on the same primitive sample space.

For the general selector-changing interaction decomposition, which introduces the intermediate cross term $Q(U_1,S_0)$, we additionally assume
\[
\mathbb E_\mu[|U_1|S_0]<\infty.
\]
When $S_1=S_0$, this cross-integrability condition is already included in the state-1 weighted-integrability assumption above.
